## AIRS Standard v1.1 --- Introduction and Principles

**Status:** V1.1

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Previous version:** V1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Type of change:** Operational (addition of principle and documentary reference; does not modify rights or eligibility)

### 1. Purpose

AIRS is a regenerative reputation system designed to recognize a verifiable track record of positive decisions, contributions and actions. AIRS Points summarize reputation; Badges preserve the history that built it.

**We protect the present. We correct the past. We build the future.**

### 2. Guiding principles

#### Reputation before reward

AIRS represent verified contributions. An eventual financial reward does not define the legitimacy of the action.

#### Evidence before emission

Any AIRS material allocation must be associated with an eligible action and sufficient evidence.

#### Career and legacy

Everyday Regenerative Actions build a trajectory. Regenerative Missions can build legacy.

#### Regenerative Risk

Proactive actions can fail. AIRS recognizes legitimate and verifiable contribution, it does not guarantee the subsequent result.

#### Reputational sustainability

Missions, campaigns and recurring rules may operate with AIRS Budgets, limits and identifiable sources of financing or value generation.

#### Predictability

Before acting, the user must be able to know or reasonably estimate the AIRS that may be obtained and its validation conditions.

#### Non-transferability

AIRS belong to the reputation of the participant. They are not bought, sold or transferred between users.

#### Traceability

Each increment must record source, reason, date and reference sufficient for audit.

#### Neutrality towards the result

A correctly made contribution does not lose legitimacy because a Mission does not achieve its future objectives.

#### No financial promise

AIRS, Badges and Missions do not constitute an investment, debt, deposit or promise of profitability.

#### Reputation and active participation are different variables

The AIRS balance summarizes a participant's historical trajectory and never decreases due to inactivity. Active and recent participation with the economic sustainability of the ecosystem is measured separately through the **RBI Participation Factor (RPF)**, which can increase or decrease. Both variables fulfill different functions and are combined only for the purposes of calculating the weight of a user in an RBI distribution (see document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_).

### 3. Conceptual architecture

- **Conservation Missions:** protect the present.

- **Compensation Missions:** allow us to assume responsibility for impacts.

- **Proactive Missions:** build future regenerative capacity.

- **Everyday Regenerative Actions:** turn recurring decisions into a trajectory.

- **Badges:** preserve memory.

- **AIRS:** quantifies accumulated reputation.

- **Status:** recognizes participation levels and, in accordance with the Status and Multipliers Regulations, adjusts the user's RBI Participation Factor.

- **RBI Participation Factor (RPF):** measures the active and recent participation of the user with the economic sustainability of the ecosystem.

- **RBI:** distributes, where they exist, available resources under separate collective rules, using AIRS and the RPF as combined variables.

### 1. Object

These Terms and Conditions regulate access, use and participation in AIRS, a regenerative reputation system that recognizes verifiable actions carried out by people and organizations within the Alternun ecosystem.

AIRS seeks to make visible a trajectory of positive decisions and contributions through AIRS Points, Badges, Regenerative Missions, Daily Regenerative Actions and other recognition mechanisms.

The philosophy of the Program can be summarized as follows:

**We protect the present. We correct the past. We build the future.**

### 2. Nature of AIRS

AIRS is a reputation, participation and loyalty system. AIRS Points are internal, personal and non-transferable units that allow you to record and summarize verified contributions within the Program.

AIRS Points:

1. do not constitute legal tender;

2. do not constitute deposits;

3. do not constitute shares, quotas, corporate interests or property rights over Alternun, its allies or the projects;

4. do not constitute debt securities;

5. do not grant a right of credit against Alternun;

6. are not transferable between users;

7. are not freely negotiable;

8. they do not promise profitability, appreciation or return of capital; and

9. do not represent in themselves an acquired right to receive RBI.

AIRS are obtained for eligible and verified actions in accordance with the current rules of the Program. A user's active and recent involvement with the economic sustainability of the ecosystem is measured separately by the **RBI Participation Factor (RPF)** (see Section 14 and the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document).

### 3. Reputation principle

AIRS is not intended to reduce a person's share to a balance. The AIRS balance summarizes a reputation; Badges and associated records preserve the history that gave rise to it.

Two participants with an equivalent AIRS balance may have built completely different trajectories, and even have a different RPF based on their recent participation.

### 4. Forms of participation

The Program recognizes, among others, two main forms of participation:

#### 4.1 Regenerative Missions

They are initiatives organized around a specific and previously defined objective. They may require economic resources, labor, knowledge, logistics, technology or other contributions.

The Missions are mainly classified as:

**a. Conservation Missions — we protect the present.**

They seek to protect, maintain, recover or care for existing environmental, social or community assets.

**b. Clearing Missions — we correct the past.**

They allow a person or organization to recognize an impact attributable to an activity and allocate resources to an eligible action or project aimed at compensating, mitigating it or contributing to its repair.

**c. Proactive Missions — we build the future.**

They seek to create new regenerative, environmental, social or economic capacity. They may include productive infrastructure, renewable energy, regenerative supply chains, sustainable agriculture, circular economy or other initiatives.

When a Mission or economic action meets the categorization criteria by type of contribution to the RBI fund (Type 1, Type 2 or Type 3), its particular file will expressly indicate this in accordance with the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

#### 4.2 Everyday Regenerative Actions

They are recurring and verifiable behaviors that shift consumption or participation decisions towards alternatives aligned with the regenerative economy, for example purchases made from eligible partners, use of sustainable services or other actions approved by the Program.

Everyday actions build the **Regenerative Trajectory**. Missions can contribute to **Regenerative Legacy**.

### 5. Eligible contributions

A person may build AIRS through one or more of the following ways, when they have been previously enabled and subsequently verified:

- financial contributions;

- physical work;

- professional work;

- volunteering;

- mentoring;

- specialized knowledge;

- logistics or transportation;

- equipment, materials or infrastructure;

- participation in conservation actions;

- compensations;

- participation in Proactive Missions;

- Daily Regenerative Actions;

- governance, education or other expressly eligible ecosystem actions.

The existence of a contribution does not automatically imply the allocation of AIRS. The action must meet the applicable eligibility, evidence, and validation rules. When the contribution is economic, its classification as Type 1, Type 2 or Type 3 will determine, in addition to the applicable AIRS rate, whether it can affect the user's RPF, in accordance with the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### 6. Financial budget and AIRS Budget

Each Mission will be able to count on:

1. a **Financial Budget**, intended to finance its execution; and

2. an **AIRS Budget**, which establishes the maximum AIRS that the Mission is authorized to recognize, unless modified duly approved in accordance with the Program rules.

The AIRS Budget may be distributed among different roles and types of contribution.

The existence of an AIRS Budget seeks to protect the sustainability, traceability and coherence of the Program and avoid arbitrary allocations.

### 7. Sustainability principle

Any AIRS material allocation must be linked to an eligible, identifiable and verifiable action and, where applicable, to a source of funding, sponsorship or value generation defined for the corresponding Mission, campaign, alliance or action rule.

Alternun may establish limits, budgets, rates, multipliers and control mechanisms intended to prevent abuse, farming, fraud or disproportionate dilution of reputation within the ecosystem.

### 8. Financial contributions

When a Mission admits financial contributions, the participant contributes resources to make the objective described in the Mission's particular file possible.

The financial contribution does not constitute, by itself:

- investment in the project;

- loan;

- deposit;

- acquisition of shareholding;

- right over project assets;

- right on income, profits or surpluses of the project; nor

- promise to return the contribution, unless the particular form expressly establishes a reimbursement mechanism permitted by law.

The reward offered may include a Badge and the allocation of AIRS according to previously known rules.

### 9. Contributions of time, work and knowledge

AIRS may recognize non-monetary contributions when they have been authorized, registered and verified.

The activity sheet must indicate, when applicable:

- nature of the task;

- validation manager;

- unit of measurement;

- AIRS base;

- boundaries;

- evidence required; and

- security and participation conditions.

The allocation of AIRS for a contribution does not convert such activity into an employment relationship. However, AIRS may not be used to conceal, substitute or avoid salaries, benefits, fees or legal obligations when, due to actual circumstances, a paid employment, contractual or professional relationship exists.

### 10. Sponsors and allies

A Daily Regenerative Mission or Action may be fully or partially financed by Alternun, companies, organizations, public entities, foundations, businesses, users or other eligible allies.

Sponsoring a Mission does not grant the sponsor the power to alter evidence, validate non-existent contributions, or assign AIRS outside of approved rules.

### 11. Badges

Badges are digital recognitions associated with facts, actions, Missions or verifiable milestones in the participant's career.

A Badge can contain or link information about the action taken, date, project, role, evidence, category, expected impact and subsequent evolution of the initiative.

**AIRS sums up the reputation. Badges preserve their history.**

Some Badges may be dynamic or "Living Badges", so that they incorporate subsequent information about the development of the Mission without retroactively modifying the historical contribution of the participant.

The file for each Badge will indicate its technological nature and the rights it incorporates. The mere existence of a Badge does not confer economic rights over a Mission or project.

### 12. Digital assets and badges issued on blockchain

When a Badge is issued through blockchain, NFT, non-fungible token, verifiable credential or other digital technology, its main function within AIRS will be to accredit or represent a participation, recognition or digital memory according to the particular issuance rules.

Unless expressly and legally valid indication to the contrary, said digital asset does not represent participation in the capital, debt, ownership of physical assets, profits from a project or right to profitability.

The technology used to register a Badge does not itself change the economic nature of the underlying relationship.

### 13. Regenerative Basic Income — RBI

The RBI is a separate value distribution mechanism within the Alternun ecosystem.

The resources available to RBI may come from sources defined by the ecosystem, including allocations of income or surpluses actually received from projects, alliances or other activities.

The RBI:

- is not salary;

- it is not guaranteed income;

- does not constitute interest on a contribution;

- does not constitute a dividend from a Mission;

- does not constitute promised consideration for acquiring a Badge;

- does not constitute a return of the contribution made to a Mission; and

\*depends on the effective existence of available resources and the current eligibility and distribution rules.

Participating in a Mission, acquiring a Badge, earning AIRS, or maintaining a high RPF **does not guarantee that the participant will receive RBI**.

### 14. AIRS, RPF and RBI distribution

When the current rules use AIRS to determine the proportionality of an RBI distribution, the variable used is not the raw AIRS balance, but the **Effective AIRS**, the result of combining the user's AIRS balance with their current **RBI Participation Factor (RPF)**, including the applicable Status multiplier in accordance with the Status and Multipliers Regulation.

The reference formula, developed in detail in the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document, is:

```text
RPF ajustado   = mínimo( RPF base × Multiplicador de Status , 100% )
Effective AIRS = AIRS acumulados × RPF ajustado
```

A user's relative participation may change as other participants build AIRS or modify their own RPF. Therefore, obtaining AIRS today, or a high RPF today, does not establish a future rate, percentage, performance or specific economic right.

Any eventual distribution will depend, among other factors, on:

- existence of resources in the corresponding fund;

- current rules;

- Effective AIRS of each user (AIRS × adjusted RPF);

- AIRS Total eligible system personnel;

- activity or verification requirements; and

- other conditions applicable at the time of distribution.

The RPF is a participation variable independent of the AIRS balance: a low or 0% RPF does not eliminate the user's AIRS history, but reduces its relative weight within an RBI distribution as long as it remains at that level.

### 15. Regenerative Risk Principle

Proactive Missions can finance initiatives whose economic, environmental or social outcome is uncertain.

The subsequent success of an initiative is not a condition to validate a contribution made legitimately.

Therefore, a Mission may not generate income, fail to achieve the expected impact, or even fail without retroactively invalidating the AIRS and Badges correctly assigned for contributions actually made.

The Program recognizes the willingness to contribute to the construction of regenerative solutions, not future profitability.

## AIRS — Definitions

**Version:** 1.1

**Previous version:** 1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Exchange rate:** Economic/reputational (new terms derived from the RBI Participation Factor)

The following definitions are used in the AIRS standard documents. When a particular sheet establishes a more specific meaning, this will apply only within said sheet as long as it does not contradict the general nature of the Program.

### Eligible Action

Conduct, contribution, transaction or activity previously enabled by AIRS that can generate recognition after being verified.

### Everyday Regenerative Action

Recurrent and verifiable behavior that guides consumption or participation decisions towards alternatives aligned with the regenerative economy.

### Action Type 1

Economic action whose payment must be used mainly to execute or finance an external restoration or compensation intervention. Generates 5 AIRS per eligible USD and does not generate RPF Boost. Defined in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### Action Type 2

Economic action whose payment already constitutes, in itself, a positive activity for the environment or the community, so its surplus can directly strengthen the RBI fund. Generates 10 AIRS per eligible USD and, when Qualified Action, an RPF Boost of +2.5 percentage points. Defined in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### Action Type 3

Productive regenerative action linked to a project that assumes a commitment to allocate future profits or surpluses to the ecosystem or the RBI fund. Generates 20 AIRS per eligible USD and, when Qualified Action, an RPF Boost of +25 percentage points. Defined in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### Activity Floor

Minimum impairment limit for a user's RBI Participation Factor, determined by their overall AIRS activity (including Type 1 shares), in accordance with the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document. It is not a recovery mechanism for RPF already lost.

### AIRS

Alternun regenerative reputation system and, depending on context, internal unit used to quantify verified contributions. AIRS is not a transferable token or currency.

### AIRS Activity Clock

Counter that records the time since the last action that generated valid AIRS of any type for a user, used to determine the applicable Activity Floor.

### Effective AIRS

Result of multiplying a user's accumulated AIRS balance by their adjusted RBI Participation Factor (including the Status multiplier). It is the variable used to calculate the relative weight of a user within an RBI distribution. See document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### AIRS Ledger

Cumulative record of AIRS assignments and their evidence, designed to preserve traceability, source, reason, date and reference of each increase.

### AIRS Base

Amount of AIRS corresponding to a share before applying a multiplier.

### AIRS Granted

Final amount recorded after applying current rules and multipliers.

### Ally

Legal person, organization, business, entity or project authorized to participate in a Mission, finance AIRS, verify actions or offer Everyday Regenerative Actions.

### Badge

Digital recognition associated with an action, Mission, milestone or verifiable contribution. Preserves part of the history of the participant's reputation.

### Living Badge

Badge whose information can be later enriched with data on the evolution of the Mission or associated impact, without altering the original historical contribution.

### Beneficiary

Person, community, organization, ecosystem or territory that receives or may receive positive effects derived from a Mission.

### Compensation

Action through which a person or organization recognizes an impact attributable to an activity and allocates resources to an eligible initiative aimed at compensating, mitigating it or contributing to its repair. It does not in itself imply a certification of neutrality.

### Economic Contribution

Monetary contribution intended to finance a Mission or other eligible action.

### Non-Monetary Contribution

Contribution of time, work, knowledge, equipment, materials, logistics, infrastructure or other resources other than money.

### Verified Contribution

Contribution whose completion has been verified in accordance with the Verification Regulations.

### Curatorship

Process through which AIRS evaluates the eligibility of projects, allies, activities or rules before incorporating them into the ecosystem.

### Regenerative Economy

Economic model aimed not only at reducing damage but also at strengthening, restoring or creating long-term environmental, social and economic capacity.

### Evidence

Information sufficient to reasonably demonstrate that an Eligible Action occurred.

### RBI Fund

Set of resources effectively available and allocated to the RBI mechanism in accordance with current rules.

### Regenerative Legacy

Long-term footprint associated with what a person or organization helped protect, repair or build through Missions and verified contributions.

### Regenerative Mission

Initiative organized around a specific objective, with rules, responsible parties, verification mechanisms and, when applicable, Financial Budget and AIRS Budget.

### Conservation Mission

Mission aimed at protecting, maintaining, recovering or caring for existing environmental, social or community assets. Narratively it represents the present.

### Clearing Mission

Mission aimed at channeling resources towards eligible actions related to impacts recognized by participants. Narratively it represents responsibility for the past.

### Proactive Mission

Mission aimed at creating new future regenerative capacity. It may involve risk and uncertain results. Narratively it represents the future.

### Multiplier

Factor applicable to certain categories of AIRS according to campaign, mission or other authorized rules. It should not be confused with the Status Multiplier, which is applied to the RPF and not to the accumulation of AIRS (see definition below).

### Status Multiplier

Factor defined in the Status and Multipliers Regulations that is applied to the base RBI Participation Factor (RPF) of a user, in accordance with the current Status, up to a limit of 100%. Does not apply to AIRS accumulation. See document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### Participant

Person or organization that performs an Eligible Action within AIRS.

### Sponsor

Person or organization that fully or partially finances an AIRS Mission, campaign, activity or budget.

### POSH

Signature and validation system or mechanism used by AIRS to register certain verified contributions, according to the current technological implementation.

### AIRS Budget

Maximum amount of AIRS authorized for a Mission or campaign, distributable according to previously defined rules.

### Financial Budget

Economic resources planned to execute a Mission.

### Project

Initiative, organization or real asset related to a Mission or the generation of impact within the ecosystem.

### Curated Project

Project that has exceeded the eligibility criteria defined by AIRS.

### Qualified Action

Type 2 or Type 3 economic action that meets the minimum criteria published by Alternun (minimum contribution, eligibility period, verification, absence of reversals, among others) and that, therefore, can generate an increase in the RBI Participation Factor. Defined in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### RBI Activity Clock

Counter that records the time elapsed since the last Qualified Action Type 2 or Type 3 of a user, used to determine the decay of the RBI Participation Factor.

### RBI Participation Factor (RPF)

Percentage between 0% and 100%, independent of the AIRS balance, that determines what proportion of a user's AIRS effectively participates in the calculation of their relative weight within an RBI rewards cycle. It increases through Qualified Actions Type 2 or Type 3, decreases by inactivity according to decay rules, and is protected by an Activity Floor. Defined in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### Regenerative Basic Income (RBI)

Collective distribution mechanism of effectively available resources within the ecosystem, calculated based on the Effective AIRS (AIRS × adjusted RPF) of eligible users. It is not salary, guaranteed income, interest, dividend, or promised consideration for a contribution.

### Regenerative Reputation

Cumulative representation of the verifiable trajectory of decisions and contributions of a person or organization within the ecosystem.

### AIRS Status

Recognition level achieved in accordance with the applicable accrual rules during a period. Determines, among other benefits, the Status Multiplier applicable to the user's RPF.

### Regenerative Pathway

Accumulated history of actions, decisions, Missions, contributions and Badges that allow us to understand how a person has built their reputation over time.

### Validation

Process by which evidence is reviewed and accepted for AIRS purposes.

### Validator

Person, organization, system or mechanism authorized to confirm an Eligible Action.

### Verification

Set of procedures used to establish whether an action occurred and complies with the applicable rules.

## AIRS --- Accumulation Rules

**Version:** 2.0

**Previous version:** 1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Exchange rate:** Economic/reputational (replaces the single compensation rate with the three-type economic action model)

### 1. General principle

AIRS is accumulated as a result of verifiable actions. **More real impact and more sustained engagement = more AIRS.**

AIRS is cumulative. Once correctly recognized, it remains in the history unless rectified due to fraud, duplicity or error.

### 2. Fundamental rules

1. AIRS is not purchased directly.

2. AIRS is not transferred or sold.

3. AIRS does not burn as an ordinary economic mechanism.

4. AIRS is earned by eligible and verified actions.

5. The rules must be public and reasonably predictable.

6. An action may be subject to AIRS Budget, limits or quotas.

7. AIRS do not guarantee RBI.

8. The legitimacy of a contribution does not depend on the future success of the Mission.

### 3. AIRS for economic shares: Type 1, Type 2 and Type 3

Ecosystem economic stocks are classified into three categories based on how they contribute to the RBI fund. The AIRS rates, payment target percentages and the eventual effect on the RBI Participation Factor (RPF) of each type are defined in the document **AIRS — RBI Participation Factor**, which is the sole source for these rules.

**Classification rule (common sense):**

- If the payment must be used mainly to **execute or finance an external intervention** of restoration or compensation → **Type 1**.

- If the payment **already constitutes, in itself, a positive activity** for the environment or the community, without the need for additional intervention → **Type 2**.

- If the project entails a **commitment to transfer future profits or surpluses** to the RBI system or fund → **Type 3**.

Summary of current rates (see document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ for details of splits, Qualified Actions and RPF boost):

```text
Tipo 1 — Compensación o restauración:                5 AIRS por USD elegible
Tipo 2 — Actividad positiva que fortalece el RBI:    10 AIRS por USD elegible
Tipo 3 — Acción productiva con excedentes futuros:   20 AIRS por USD elegible
```

Only Type 2 and Type 3 actions can generate an increase in RPF, and only when they meet the Qualified Action criteria. Type 1 actions generate AIRS and count as activity for Activity Floor purposes, but do not generate RPF Boost.

### 4. AIRS by Regenerative Missions

Each Mission will have a published AIRS Budget and distribution rules. AIRS may recognize financial contributions, labor, knowledge, logistics, materials, coordination or other verifiable contributions.

When a Mission accepts financial contributions, its record must indicate whether these are classified as Type 1, Type 2 or Type 3 in accordance with Section 3.

In a Proactive Mission, AIRS are recognized for the contribution made and are not conditioned to future profits or results. Proactive Missions with commitment of future surpluses to the RBI are, as a general rule, the typical scenario for a Type 3 action.

### 5. Economic contribution

The particular form will establish the relationship between contribution and AIRS before participating, including its classification as Type 1, 2 or 3 when applicable. The contribution finances the Mission; AIRS is reputational recognition and not participation in the project.

There may be campaign or mission boosts (on AIRS) for priority Missions if they are previously defined and respect the AIRS Budget. These boosts are different from the Status Multiplier, which applies to the RPF and not to the accumulation of AIRS (see Status and Multipliers Regulations).

### 6. Work and direct contribution

Work contributions must be assigned, recorded and validated.

**Initial reference rule: 20 AIRS per validated hour.**

The rate may vary by category, specialty, Mission or AIRS Budget. Hours may require signature via POSH and may be subject to caps.

Validated work is not classified as Type 1, 2 or 3 and, therefore, does not generate RPF Boost, although it does count as AIRS activity for Activity Floor purposes.

### 7. Participation in eligible projects

When participation or permanence mechanisms are enabled in curated projects, their rules may recognize AIRS for sustained commitment. Any underlying assets or tokens must be kept legally separate from AIRS.

When such participation corresponds to a project with a commitment of future surpluses to the RBI, it may be classified as Type 3 in accordance with Section 3 and the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

### 8. Everyday Regenerative Actions

Allies will be able to finance recurring rules: eligible Fruver purchases, eco-hotels, reused goods, eligible products with recycled materials or other approved actions.

Each rule will indicate rate, source of financing, evidence, limits and validity. Unless Alternun expressly classifies a rule as Type 2 or Type 3, Everyday Regenerative Actions do not generate RPF Boost.

### 9. Structural participation

Fixed and low-weight AIRS may be awarded for onboarding, eligible voting, education, official campaigns, verified references or events. They should not displace sources based on real impact. These actions do not generate RPF Boost.

### 10. Campaign or mission multipliers

Campaign or mission multipliers amplify base AIRS in eligible categories. They are not normally applied to trivial actions and do not accumulate with each other unless expressly stated. These multipliers are independent of the Status Multiplier, which affects the RPF and not the accumulation of AIRS (see Status and Multipliers Regulations and document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_).

### 11. AIRS budget

A Mission or campaign may not assign more AIRS than those authorized, unless a formal extension is registered. Unused budget should not be issued.

### 12. Corrections

Errors, duplicities, fraud or invalid evidence may be rectified in an auditable manner.

### 13. Future changes

Rates and parameters may evolve in accordance with the Governance Regulations. The actions carried out are evaluated under the rules in force at the time of their execution, except for fraud or error. Changes to Type 1/2/3 rates and their associated RPF rules are managed in the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document.

## AIRS --- Verification and Evidence Regulations

**Version:** 1.1

**Previous version:** 1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Exchange type:** Operational (incorporates verification of Qualified Actions for RPF purposes)

### 1. Object

Defines how AIRS determines that an action occurred and meets the conditions necessary to be part of the reputation.

### 2. Principle

**There is no verifiable reputation without verifiable evidence.**

The level of evidence will be proportional to the type of action, value, fraud risk and AIRS involved.

### 3. States

An action may be Registered, Pending, Validated, Rejected, Observed or Rectified. Only a validated action generates definitive AIRS.

### 4. Minimum registration

Each entry will attempt to retain userId, timestamp, category, actionType, missionId/ruleId, baseAirs, multiplierApplied, totalAirsGranted, referenceId, referenceType, validator, evidence, txHash where applicable, and relevant metadata. When the action is Type 2 or Type 3, the entry must also record whether it meets the Qualified Action criteria and, if so, the RPF Boost granted.

### 5. Evidence

Proof of payment, POS, API, invoices, photographs, georeferencing when necessary and authorized, signatures, POSH, certifications, minutes, sensors, blockchain, documents or human validation may be used. No one type of evidence will be universally sufficient on its own.

### 6. Financial contributions

The participant's account, amount, Mission, payment, absence of reversal when applicable and applicable AIRS rule will be confirmed.

#### 6.1 Additional verification for Qualified Actions (Type 2 and Type 3)

When the financial contribution is Type 2 or Type 3 and the mission, project or campaign supports RPF Boost, the verification must confirm, in addition to what is indicated in Section 6, the **Qualified Action** criteria defined in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_:

- minimum eligible contribution, when applicable;

- current eligibility period;

- identity or transaction verification;

- minimum permanence, when necessary to execute the action;

- absence of reversals or refunds at the time of granting the RPF Boost;

- if the transaction must be grouped with other related transactions because they correspond to the same economic operation, to avoid artificial fractioning.

A Type 2 or Type 3 action that does not meet these criteria may continue to generate AIRS at the applicable rate, but will not generate RPF Boost.

### 7. Work and time

The hours must correspond to eligible activity, be assigned or accepted, have responsibility, reasonable duration and POSH support or other approved mechanism when applicable.

### 8. In-person conservation

Attendance, signature, photographic evidence, coordinator, general location and basic result may be required, minimizing personal data.

### 9. Compensations

A distinction will be made between impact statement, calculation, contribution, selected project and evidence of application. Without formal certification, AIRS will not present the action as certified neutrality.

### 10. Proactive Missions

The contribution is validated, not the future result. The subsequent failure of an initiative does not invalidate a legitimate contribution, nor does it reverse a legitimately granted Type 3 RPF Boost.

### 11. Everyday Actions

Automatic auditable integrations will be preferred: POS, API, invoice or transaction code. The ally cannot self-assign AIRS outside of authorized rules, nor classify an action as Type 2 or Type 3 on its own.

### 12. Validators

There may be authorization levels, limits, double validation, audits and conflict of interest rules.

### 13. P.O.S.H.

POSH may sign contributions of work, presence or other activities, reasonably linking participant, activity, date, Mission and person responsible.

### 14. Fraud

Duplicity, reused evidence, collusion, false identities, location manipulation, reverse transactions, artificial creation of AIRS and artificial fractioning of the same economic operation will be controlled to obtain multiple increases in RPF.

### 15. Audit

The system must allow reconstruction of why a person received AIRS and, where applicable, why they did or did not receive an RPF Boost. Any rectification will leave history.

### 16. Privacy

Traceability does not imply that all evidence is public. Only what is necessary in accordance with the data policy will be kept.

### 17. Review

The participant may have a reasonable review mechanism when an action is rejected or rectified, including the rejection of an RPF Boost for not meeting the Qualified Action criteria.

## AIRS --- Status and Multipliers

**Version:** 2.0

**Previous version:** 1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Exchange rate:** Economic/reputational (Status multiplier changes its function: it is now applied to the RBI Participation Factor and not to the accumulation of AIRS)

### 1. Purpose

AIRS Status recognizes consistency and depth of participation. It does not represent wealth, solvency or financial status.

### 2. Levels

| Status   | AIRS accumulated in the calendar year | Role                    |
| -------- | ------------------------------------: | ----------------------- |
| Green    |                                  Base | Entry / discovery       |
| Silver   |                                 3,000 | Active participation    |
| Gold     |                                 7,000 | Consistent taxpayer     |
| Platinum |                                12,000 | Structural support      |
| Diamond  |                                18,000 | Pillar of the ecosystem |

The AIRS names and thresholds to achieve each Status may change at any time in accordance with the Governance Regulations. These thresholds remain unchanged in this release - the market and actual accumulation speed will determine whether they warrant adjustment in the future.

### 3. Status multiplier on the RBI Participation Factor (RPF)

Starting with this version, the Status multiplier **no longer applies to AIRS accumulation**. It applies exclusively to the user's **RBI Participation Factor (RPF)**, in accordance with the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document, which is the sole source of this rule.

| Status   | Multiplier on RPF |
| -------- | ----------------: |
| Green    |             1.00x |
| Silver   |             1.02x |
| Gold     |             1.05x |
| Platinum |             1.08x |
| Diamond  |             1.12x |

```text
RPF ajustado = mínimo( RPF base × Multiplicador de Status , 100% )
```

Example: A Silver user with base RPF of 50% gets an adjusted RPF of 51% (50% × 1.02). See document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_, Section 10, for complete detail and more examples.

### 4. Application

1. The multiplier is applied at the time of calculating the user's weight for an RBI cycle, not at the time of an individual AIRS accumulation action.

2. It is not retroactive.

3. The Status in force at that moment is used.

4. When ascending from Status, the new multiplier applies from that moment forward.

5. Status multipliers do not stack with each other.

6. The Status multiplier does not modify the limits of increase (+25 pp/quarter), decay (−25 pp/6 months without Type 2/3) or the Activity Floor of the base RPF: these limits are always calculated on the base RPF, before applying the multiplier.

7. The adjusted RPF never exceeds 100%, regardless of the user's Status or base RPF.

### 5. AIRS campaign or mission multipliers

Regardless of the Status multiplier, there may be campaign or mission multipliers that amplify the accumulation of base AIRS in eligible categories, in accordance with the Accumulation Rules (Section 10). These AIRS multipliers are different and do not replace the Status Multiplier described in Section 3, which acts exclusively on the RPF.

### 6. Status Validity

Status is obtained immediately upon reaching the threshold and is maintained for the rest of the year.

At the beginning of the following year, the user initially retains the benefits of the Status achieved in the previous year, while the annual counter resets. If you reach a higher level, it applies from that moment on.

### 7. Lifetime status

When a participant has achieved or exceeded a certain Status for at least ten years, that level may become their initial lifetime Status. The years do not need to be consecutive.

If you reach Silver or higher for ten years, Silver can become your base. If you subsequently complete ten years at Gold or higher, Gold can become your new base.

### 8. Protection

The Status multiplier adjusts the user's RPF, but does not guarantee an RBI reward or a certain amount: it still depends on the resources actually available in each cycle (see AIRS — RBI and Reward Distribution). Additional caps may exist in categories at risk of abuse.

### 9. Changes

AIRS Thresholds for Status may be modified for future periods in accordance with the Governance Regulations and must be published before coming into force. Changes to the Status multiplier on the RPF are managed and published in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

## AIRS — RBI and Reward Distribution

**Version:** 1.2

**Previous version:** 1.1

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Exchange rate:** Economic/reputational (the distribution formula changes from using eligible AIRS to using Effective AIRS, incorporating the RBI Participation Factor)

### 1. Purpose

**Regenerative Basic Income (RBI)** is the rewards mechanism of the AIRS ecosystem.

Unlike traditional loyalty programs, where the reward is usually directly associated with a transaction - for example, points, miles or cashback - in AIRS the reward is determined primarily based on the participant's **accumulated reputation** and **active and recent participation** within the ecosystem.

AIRS records reputation based on verifiable actions. The **RBI Participation Factor (RPF)** measures active and recent participation. The RBI uses both variables combined – the **Effective AIRS** – as a basis to determine each user's relative participation in the available rewards.

By design, RBI rewards will be distributed **primarily in ATN**, without prejudice to the ecosystem may distribute other species, benefits, digital assets, products, services, experiences or other rewards when there are specific campaigns, alliances or promotions.

### 2. Fundamental principle

**Reputation is built first. Active participation then determines how much of that reputation counts. Finally, when rewards are available, they are distributed according to RBI rules.**

Holding AIRS, even with a high RPF, does not in itself imply that a reward is available in a given period.

The RBI should not be construed as a promise of income, profitability or economic return. It is a **variable reward mechanism**, conditional on the existence of resources or benefits available within the ecosystem.

### 3. Nature of the reward

RBI rewards can come from different sources in the ecosystem and take different forms.

The primary form intended for distribution is **ATN**.

There may also be rewards in other species, for example:

- products;

- services;

- discounts;

- experiences;

- benefits offered by allies;

- digital assets;

- special accesses;

- targeted promotions;

- other rewards enabled by the ecosystem.

These rewards may be general or targeted to specific segments of users based on criteria of reputation, track record, location, participation, activity category or other previously communicated conditions.

### 4. Sources of rewards

The rewards system may be drawn from authorized sources, including:

- platform revenue allocations;

- contributions from the Compensation Pool and Type 2 shares;

- surpluses from Type 3 projects when there is an agreement;

- contributions from allies;

- promotional campaigns;

- subsidies;

- institutional contributions;

\*other approved sources.

A potential source is not considered available until the resource or benefit has actually been received, enabled, or made available to the reward mechanism.

### 5. AIRS, RPF and reputational basis

AIRS is not the reward. AIRS is the **reputation measure** that allows the ecosystem to recognize how much and how a person has participated over time.

The **RBI Participation Factor (RPF)** measures, separately, how active and recent that person's participation has been with the economic sustainability of the ecosystem, according to the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

The RBI uses the combined result of both variables – the **Effective AIRS** – to determine the relative participation of users in the available rewards.

**Verified actions → AIRS → RPF (active participation) → Effective AIRS → Eligibility and proportionality → RBI Rewards **

### 6. Eligibility

The operational version of the RBI may require:

\*active account;

- identity or verification where applicable;

- AIRS minimum threshold;

- an RPF greater than zero, or other minimum RPF threshold that is published for the cycle;

- compliance with ecosystem rules;

- recent participation;

- claim within the period;

- other previously published requirements.

Eligibility may vary depending on the nature of the reward. A user with RPF of 0% retains his AIRS balance, but his weight in the cycle (Section 7) will also be 0% until he recovers RPF.

### 7. Reference proportional distribution

When a reward is distributed proportionally, the system uses the **Effective AIRS** of each user, calculated according to the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_:

```text
RPF ajustado = mínimo( RPF base × Multiplicador de Status , 100% )
Effective AIRS = AIRS acumulados × RPF ajustado

Recompensa del usuario = (Effective AIRS del usuario / Effective AIRS totales elegibles) × Recompensa distribuible
```

This formula expresses reputational proportionality and active participation; It does not represent a financial return.

### 8. Relative dilution and variation of RPF

The AIRS accumulated by a user can remain constant while the total AIRS Eligible Cash in the ecosystem increases. Consequently, a user's relative participation in a future distribution may decrease.

Additionally, a user's weight can change between cycles due to variations in their own RPF: a decline due to inactivity reduces their Effective AIRS even if their AIRS balance does not change, and a recovery through Qualified Actions can increase them.

No AIRS allocation, no RPF level and no Status set a perpetual rate, minimum percentage or guaranteed right on future rewards.

### 9. Proactive Missions and Type 3 actions

If a Proactive Mission classified as Type 3, such as a Fruver or a solar farm, does not generate surpluses, the AIRS obtained from legitimate contributions remain as part of the participant's reputation, and the RPF Boost already granted by a Qualified Action is also not reversed.

If a Mission generates surpluses and part of them is allocated to the RBI in accordance with the commitment made, these enter the collective reward mechanism and are distributed according to the general rules in force.

The person who participated in a Mission does not therefore receive an individual right to the surpluses of said Mission.

### 10. Rewards mainly in ATN

ATN is, by design, the RBI's primary reward species.

However, the RBI is not limited exclusively to ATN.

Allies, companies or other organizations may fund or contribute additional, general or targeted rewards, as long as their nature is clearly communicated and they are not presented as a guaranteed return.

### 11. Targeted Rewards and Promotions

Companies or allies will be able to create reward campaigns aimed at specific groups of users.

Examples:

- reward for Gold users or higher;

- benefit for participants of a specific Mission;

- promotion for users from a region;

- reward for those who have completed a specific path;

- reward conditional on maintaining a minimum RPF;

- products or services delivered by an ally.

These campaigns may use AIRS, RPF, Badges, Status, trajectory or other ecosystem criteria to determine eligibility.

### 12. Periodicity

The RBI may operate through monthly cuts or other published periodicity.

Each period must identify, when applicable:

- rewards available;

- kind of reward;

- fountain;

- eligible users;

- AIRS Eligible Effective (AIRS × Adjusted RPF);

- formula or criterion;

- cutoff date;

- claim window;

- rules applicable to unclaimed rewards.

### 13. Claim

When a reward requires a claim, the user must do so within the corresponding window.

### 14. Transparency

AIRS will seek to publish sufficient aggregate information to understand what rewards are available, where they come from, what species are distributed, who is eligible, and what methodology is used, including how Effective AIRS are calculated.

### 15. Communication language

The RBI should always be communicated as a **reward mechanism**.

Interfaces, commercial documents and communications must avoid expressions that could lead the RBI to interpret such as:

- investment;

- performance;

- profitability;

- interest;

- dividend;

- return of capital;

- guaranteed income;

- economic promise.

The word **reward** will be the guiding communication concept.

### 16. Changes

RBI rules may be adjusted to preserve sustainability, clarity, regulatory compliance and consistency with the reputation system.

Material modifications must be published before coming into effect and cannot retroactively transform a past contribution into a financial promise. Changes to the Effective AIRS formula, RPF or Status multiplier on RPF are managed in the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document.

## AIRS — Regenerative Missions Regulations

**Version:** 1.1

**Previous version:** 1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Type of change:** Operational (incorporates Type 1/2/3 classification and Qualified Action fields to the Mission sheet)

### 1. Purpose

Regenerative Missions convert a collective intention into an organized, verifiable and narratively understandable action.

Each Mission must answer one of three questions:

- **What do we want to protect today?**

- **What impact do we want to assume and compensate?**

- **What do we want to help build for tomorrow?**

### 2. Categories

#### Conservation — Present

**We protect the present.**

Examples: beach cleaning, stream recovery, forest care, specific restoration, wildlife protection, guided bird watching in conservation territory, or maintenance of community infrastructure linked to a regenerative objective.

When the participant's payment already constitutes, in itself, a positive activity for the environment or the community (for example, a conservation experience such as bird watching), the financial contribution may be classified as **Action Type 2**, so that the surplus directly strengthens the RBI fund, according to the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

#### Compensation — Past

**We correct the past.**

The participant identifies an impact—for example, a trip—and selects an eligible initiative through which they wish to contribute to offsetting it.

The platform may offer calculators or methodologies. When there is no recognized certification, the experience should not be presented as certified climate neutrality or as a carbon credit.

When the participant's payment must be used primarily to execute or finance the restoration or compensation intervention, the financial contribution is classified as **Action Type 1**, according to the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

#### Proactive — Future

**We build the future.**

Examples: Fruver, solar farms, circular infrastructure, regenerative agriculture, inclusive supply chains or projects capable of creating new environmental, social or economic capacity.

These Missions can fail. AIRS recognizes verified contribution and willingness to build; does not promise the result.

When the Mission operator assumes a commitment to allocate future profits or surpluses to the ecosystem or the RBI fund, the economic contribution is classified as **Action Type 3**, according to the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_.

#### Note on economic classification

The narrative category of the Mission (Conservation, Compensation or Proactive) does not automatically determine its Economic Type. Classification as Type 1, 2 or 3 depends on what happens financially to the participant's payment, in accordance with the common sense rule described in the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_, and must be expressly indicated in the Mission sheet (Section 3).

### 3. Mandatory Mission Sheet

Every Mission must have, at a minimum,:

- identifier;

- name;

- category (Conservation, Compensation or Proactive);

- type of economic action, when it admits economic contributions (Type 1, Type 2 or Type 3, in accordance with the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_);

- narrative;

- aim;

- operator/responsible;

- territory;

- start date and, if applicable, closing date;

\*eligible participants;

- roles;

- Financial Budget;

- AIRS budget;

- funding source(s);

- AIRS rules;

- when the action type is Type 2 or Type 3: Qualified Action criteria and the applicable RPF Boost;

- Badge(s);

- evidence required;

- validators;

- main risks;

- expected indicators;

- policy if goal is not achieved;

- policy on unused resources;

- updates and reports;

- closing rules.

### 4. Roles

A Mission may include:

- Sponsors;

- Operator;

- Coordinators;

- Economic participants;

- Work participants;

- Professionals or mentors;

- Validators;

- Beneficiaries;

- Allies.

The same person can play more than one role when there is no incompatible conflict of interest.

### 5. AIRS budget

The AIRS Budget is a reputational control tool.

Before opening a Mission you must define:

1. maximum authorized quantity;

2. contribution categories and, when applicable, economic type (Type 1/2/3);

3. AIRS base by category;

4. campaign or mission multipliers applicable on AIRS;

5. limits per participant;

6. reserves for contingencies, if they exist.

Unused allocations do not have to be issued.

### 6. Regenerative Risk

The future result does not retroactively determine the validity of a contribution.

A Proactive Mission that fails may have produced legitimate contributions, learning, coordination, and risk appetite. Correctly granted AIRS remain part of the trajectory, and a Type 3 RPF Boost legitimately granted by a Qualified Action is also not reversed, barring fraud or error.

Risk should be explained before participating and never become an implicit promise of return.

### 7. Project surpluses

A Mission may inform that the operator intends or is contractually obliged to allocate part of future surpluses to the ecosystem or the RBI Fund. This commitment is typically what classifies the Mission as **Action Type 3** according to the _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_ document.

This circumstance:

- does not make the participant an investor in the project;

- does not create an individual right over those surpluses;

- does not set a profitability;

- does not guarantee that there are surpluses; and

- does not link the amount of AIRS awarded, nor the RPF Boost received, to future financial performance.

### 8. Closure and memory

At the end of a Mission, the following must be recorded, to the extent reasonably possible:

- resources used;

- activities executed;

- participants;

- AIRS assigned;

\*RPF Boosts granted, when applicable;

- observed results;

- deviations;

- learnings;

- final state.

Badges will continue to receive subsequent updates on the evolution of what the community helped protect, compensate or build.

## AIRS --- Badges, Trajectory and Regenerative Memory

**Version:** 1.0

### 1. Principle

**AIRS sums up your reputation. Badges preserve their history.**

A Badge is not simply a visual award. It is a structured fragment of the regenerative history of a person or organization.

### 2. Purpose

Badges allow you to remember actions, accredit participation, reconstruct trajectory, show Missions, preserve milestones, create personalized experiences and update the subsequent impact of certain initiatives.

### 3. Types

There may be Mission, Conservation, Compensation, Proactive, contribution, milestone, Status, anniversary, ally and Living Badge Badges.

### 4. Metadata

Where applicable, it may contain badgeId, userId or pseudonymous reference, name, category, missionId/actionId, date, role, description, contribution, evidence or reference, associated AIRS, territory, validating entity, status, impact references, hash/txHash and version.

Not all metadata should be public.

### 5. Living Badges

A Living Badge preserves the historical contribution unchanged, but allows you to add information about what happened afterwards: operation of a solar farm, milestones of a Fruver, evolution of a forest, failure of a Mission or subsequent contributions to the RBI.

Updates do not alter the original AIRS or create economic rights.

### 6. Regenerative Pathway

It is the accumulated history of verified decisions and actions. It can include daily actions, compensation, Missions, work hours, professional contributions, Status, Badges and milestones.

### 7. Regenerative Legacy

It represents what the participant helped to protect, repair or build and whose effects can remain over time.

Not every Badge constitutes legacy. Legacy emerges especially from Missions and contributions with continuity or lasting impact.

### 8. Custom memories

The platform will be able to generate annual summary, anniversaries, first Missions, trajectory maps, milestones, Status evolution and experiences such as "five years ago you helped build this."

These experiences must be built with real and verifiable data.

### 9. Privacy

The user must have reasonable controls over visibility. Sensitive information, precise location, identifiable photographs and private evidence will not be made public solely because they are linked to a Badge.

### 10. Blockchain

A Badge may use NFT, verifiable credential, SBT or other technology. Technology does not automatically modify its legal nature.

When possible, public evidence, public metadata and private evidence will be separated.

### 11. Transferability

By default, Reputational Badges should be personal and non-transferable when the transfer destroys their meaning.

A different commercial or collectible Badge must be expressly identified and not confused with a reputational credential.

### 12. Historical integrity

AIRS should not rewrite the past to show only successes.

A failed Mission can remain in the trajectory as evidence of legitimate participation and regenerative risk.

An authentic reputation records both what succeeded and what was responsibly attempted.

## AIRS — Everyday Regenerative Actions

**Version:** 1.1

**Previous version:** 1.0 (August 6, 2026)

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Exchange Rate:** Operational (clarifies the relationship between Daily Actions and the RBI Participation Factor)

### 1. Purpose

Reputation is not only built on large Missions. It is also built through repeated decisions.

**Everyday actions build your trajectory. Missions build your legacy.**

### 2. Nature

An Everyday Regenerative Action is a repeatable and verifiable behavior previously approved by AIRS.

It does not need to become an independent Mission.

Potential examples:

- purchases at an allied Fruver;

- accommodation in an eligible eco-hotel;

- purchase of reused or second-hand goods;

\*purchasing eligible products made from recycled materials;

- other verified consumer or regenerative participation decisions.

Eligibility will never depend solely on a commercial claim by the ally.

### 3. Eligible Action Rule

Each recurring action must define:

- ally;

- action;

- eligibility criteria;

\*AIRS;

- source of financing;

- evidence;

- POS/API integration where applicable;

- frequency;

- boundaries;

- validity;

- exclusions.

### 4. Financing

AIRS associated with daily actions may be financed through contributions from the ally, campaigns, specific budgets or other authorized sources.

The existence of a purchase does not obligate AIRS to award points if the rule is not active or there is not sufficient financing/budget according to the published conditions.

### 5. Relationship with the RBI Participation Factor

Everyday Regenerative Actions generate AIRS and count as activity for the purposes of the user's Activity Floor, but **do not generate RPF Boost**, unless Alternun expressly classifies a specific rule as Action Type 2 or Type 3, in accordance with the document _AIRS — RBI Participation Factor_. When an everyday action rule is classified in this way, the rule sheet (Section 3) must expressly indicate this along with the applicable Qualified Action criteria.

### 6. Curatorship

AIRS may require evidence on the environmental, social or economic characteristics that justify the inclusion of an ally or product.

The curatorship may consider certifications, traceability, materials, business model, supply chain, circularity, local impact or other criteria.

### 7. Greenwashing prevention

The granting of AIRS is not equivalent to certifying that a company, product or service is comprehensively "sustainable", "green", "carbon neutral" or "regenerative".

AIRS recognizes only the Eligible Action and the specific validated criteria.

## AIRS — RBI Participation Factor (RPF)

**Version:** 1.0

**Date:** September 7, 2026

**Status:** Current

> **Document architecture note:** This document is the **sole and authoritative source** of the RBI Participation Factor (RPF) rules, economic action categories (Type 1, Type 2 and Type 3) and the calculation of Effective AIRS. The other AIRS standard documents refer to this document rather than repeating its numerical rules. Any future adjustments to rates, limits, decays, floors or the Status multiplier on the RPF must be made only here.

---

### 1. Purpose

AIRS records the trajectory of a participant. The RBI Participation Factor (RPF) measures your current and sustained participation with the economic sustainability of the ecosystem, particularly with the RBI fund.

**AIRS remembers the trajectory. The RPF reflects present participation.**

By design: AIRS never slows down due to inactivity; RPF can increase or decrease between 0% and 100%; a user can keep all his AIRS even if his RPF reaches 0%; recovering RPF requires new qualified participation, not just the passage of time or the size of a contribution.

### 2. Categories of economic action

The classification of an action as Type 1, 2 or 3 depends on what happens economically with the payment:

> - If the payment must be used mainly to **execute or finance an external restoration or compensation intervention** → **Type 1**.

> - If the payment **already constitutes, in itself, a positive activity** for the environment or the community and does not require additional intervention → **Type 2**, and the surplus can directly strengthen the RBI fund.

> - If the project entails a **commitment to transfer future profits or surpluses** to the RBI system or fund → **Type 3**.

#### 2.1 Type 1 — Compensation or restoration

Example: a user offsets the footprint of a trip and that payment is used to execute a specific restoration intervention.

- 80% of the eligible payment is used to execute the corresponding intervention; Remaining 20% ​​is distributed according to published rules.

- **5 AIRS for each eligible USD**, subject to validation and applicable campaign or mission multipliers.

- It does not generate a direct increase in the RPF.

- It counts as an AIRS activity for the purposes of the Activity Floor (Section 8).

#### 2.2 Type 2 — Activity that is already positive and strengthens the RBI fund

Example: a user pays for an experience that is, in itself, a positive action (for example, a guided bird watching in a conservation territory). As the payment does not fund additional intervention, the surplus directly strengthens the RBI fund.

- 80% of eligible payment goes to RBI fund; 20% goes to Alternun.

- **10 AIRS for each eligible USD**, subject to validation and applicable multipliers.

- Each action that meets the Qualified Action criteria (Section 6) can increase the base RPF by **+2.5 percentage points**.

#### 2.3 Type 3 — Productive regenerative action with commitment to future surpluses

Example: a project (for example, a Fruver or a solar farm) whose operator assumes a commitment to allocate future profits or surpluses to the ecosystem or the RBI fund.

- **20 AIRS for each eligible USD**, subject to validation and applicable multipliers.

- A Type 3 action that meets the Qualified Action criteria can increase the base RPF by **+25 percentage points**.

- This classification does not imply a guarantee of success, profitability or future generation of income of the project. The commitment to provide future surpluses does not require that said surpluses exist.

#### 2.4 Rate summary

```text
Tipo 1: AIRS base = USD elegibles × 5   · RPF Boost: no aplica
Tipo 2: AIRS base = USD elegibles × 10  · RPF Boost: +2,5 pp por Qualified Action
Tipo 3: AIRS base = USD elegibles × 20  · RPF Boost: +25 pp por Qualified Action
```

#### 2.5 Shares not classified as Type 1, 2 or 3

Validated work, Daily Regenerative Actions, onboarding, voting, referrals and education continue to generate AIRS in accordance with the Accumulation Rules, but do not generate RPF Boost unless expressly classified as Type 2 or 3.

---

### 3. What is the RPF

Percentage between 0% and 100% that determines what proportion of a user's AIRS is effectively participating in the calculation of their relative weight within an RBI rewards cycle. It is independent of the AIRS balance: a reduction in the RPF does not eliminate AIRS.

### 4. Initial RPF

Unless otherwise established by a campaign, a new eligible user starts with **base RPF = 50%**. 100% must be built through qualified participation.

### 5. RPF increase limit

Maximum **+25 percentage points per calendar quarter**, regardless of number of shares, amount contributed, Type 2/3 combination, or AIRS/Status level. From 0% to 100%, a minimum of four quarters is required; from 50% to 100%, minimum two.

### 6. Qualified Actions

To be Qualified Action, a Type 2 or 3 action must meet criteria published by Alternun: minimum contribution, eligibility period, identity/transaction verification, minimum permanence when applicable, absence of reversals, and other reasonable requirements.

**Anti-farming rules:** (1) artificial splitting into multiple payments does not generate multiple boosts — Alternun can group related transactions; (2) the same economic operation does not generate more than one RPF event unless express rule; (3) the passive holding company does not renew the RPF; (4) the maximum remains at +25pp/quarter without exception for capital volume; (5) Alternun can invalidate duplications, abusive automations or circular transactions.

### 7. Decline due to lack of productive participation

The **RBI Activity Clock** records the time since the last Qualified Action Type 2/3. After **6 months** without one, the base RPF can be reduced **25 percentage points**, repeating every additional 6 months, subject to the Activity Floor:

```text
100% → 75% → 50% → 25% → floor aplicable
```

The passive holding company does not restart this period.

### 8. Activity Floor

Measured by a separate **AIRS Activity Clock** (time since the last action that generated AIRS of any type):

| Activity status                                       | Activity Floor |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------: |
| User active through any AIRS action, including Type 1 |            15% |
| 6 months without accruing AIRS                        |            10% |
| 12 months without accumulating AIRS                   |             5% |
| 24 consecutive months without accumulating AIRS       |             0% |

The floor only limits future deterioration; does not recover RPF already lost. A new Type 1 action restores activity to the floor, but does not raise the RPF.

### 9. RPF Recovery

Type 2: up to **+2.5 pp** per Qualified Action. Type 3: up to **+25 pp** per Qualified Action. Without exceeding +25pp/quarter as a whole. Does not modify historical AIRS.

Example from 0% with Type 3: Q1 → 25%, Q2 → 50%, Q3 → 75%, Q4 → 100%.

---

### 10. Status multiplier on the RPF

The Status multiplier **does not apply to AIRS accumulation**. It applies exclusively to the **base RPF**, in accordance with the current Status and Multiplier Regulations.

| Status   | Multiplier on RPF |
| -------- | ----------------: |
| Green    |             1.00x |
| Silver   |             1.02x |
| Gold     |             1.05x |
| Platinum |             1.08x |
| Diamond  |             1.12x |

```text
RPF ajustado = mínimo( RPF base × Multiplicador de Status , 100% )
```

Example: A Silver user (1.02x) with base RPF of 50% gets an adjusted RPF of 51%. A Diamond user (1.12x) with base RPF of 80% gets 89.6%. No calculation exceeds 100%.

It is applied at the time of calculating the user's weight for an RBI cycle, using the Status in force at that moment; It is not retroactive and does not modify the increase, decay or Activity Floor limits of the base RPF (these limits are always calculated before applying the multiplier). It does not guarantee an RBI reward or a certain amount.

---

### 11. Calculation of Effective AIRS for RBI

```text
RPF ajustado   = mínimo( RPF base × Multiplicador de Status , 100% )
Effective AIRS = AIRS acumulados × RPF ajustado
User Reward Weight = User Effective AIRS / Total Eligible Effective AIRS
```

The reward is determined by applying that weight to the amount actually available in the cycle. It does not guarantee the amount or continuity of future RPF/Status.

Example: Total AIRS = 20,000; base RPF = 50%; Status Gold (1.05x) → Adjusted RPF = 52.5%; Effective AIRS = 10,500.

The RPF, with or without Status multiplier, does not eliminate or modify historical AIRS.
